## Why Unite {#intro}

[do: title-card]
This is Unite. It's a place for people to work out, together, what a good future looks like in ordinary life. And then to make sure something actually happens.

[do: open-home]
In about {minutes} minutes I'll follow one person through the whole thing: a private conversation, the picture of what everyone said, a live discussion, a decision, and what the people in charge did about it.

[do: point-loop]
Most consultation stops at collecting opinions. Unite is built around the part that comes after. Nothing is asked of you here unless somebody has already promised, in writing, to answer.

## The question, and the pledge {#pledge}

[do: open-earning]
Here's an example. The town, the council and the people in it are invented, but the problem is real. As AI changes local jobs, what would let people here keep earning, and keep learning?

[do: highlight-pledge]
This is the pledge. The council and the college have set aside a quarter of a million pounds and the college's evening timetable. They've said what's off the table, and why. And they've promised to answer the top five priorities by the eighteenth of December.

[do: highlight-stage-tracker]
Why insist on this? A study of five hundred and seventy-one proposals from local participation in Spain found only about a third were carried out in full. What made the difference was whether the people with power were behind it from the start.

## One conversation {#listen}

[do: start-conversation]
Meet Amina. She works nights in a care home. She starts where everyone starts: with a conversation, on her own, before she's seen what anyone else thinks.

[do: show-consent]
The interviewer is an AI, and it says so. It asks questions and it listens. It has no opinions, it gives no advice, and what she types stays private to her.

[do: answer-real-day]
[if: ai]
It doesn't ask what she thinks about the future of work. It asks about yesterday. Real days are more reliable than opinions.

[do: answer-real-day]
[if: !ai]
It doesn't ask what she thinks about the future of work. It asks about yesterday. Real days are more reliable than opinions. In this recording the interviewer is using fixed questions.

[do: answer-mattered]
[if: ai]
Then it picks up something she said, and asks why it mattered. One question at a time.

[do: answer-mattered]
[if: !ai]
Then it asks about one moment that mattered, and why. One question at a time.

[do: answer-expensive]
[if: ai]
Watch what happens when she says the course is too expensive. It doesn't file that under "cost". It asks when that last got in her way, and what it would let her do if it were sorted. Her words stay her words.

[do: answer-expensive]
[if: !ai]
Watch what happens when she says the course is too expensive. It doesn't file that under "cost". It asks what stops her, and she says it her way: the classes are on while she's at work or asleep. Her words stay her words.

[do: answer-good-day]
[if: ai]
Then an ordinary Tuesday, a few years from now, in a life that's going well. Not a fantasy. A Tuesday.

[do: answer-good-day]
[if: !ai]
Then an ordinary weekday, a few years from now, in a life that's going well. Not a fantasy. A weekday.

[do: show-stage-names]
There are eight short stages like this, about twelve minutes in all. She can skip any of them, or stop.

## Here is what I heard {#points}

[do: open-review]
At the end the interviewer shows her what it heard. Five points, built from her own phrases.

[do: edit-point]
She fixes one. She scraps another, and adds one of her own. She decides how each is shown: counted only, shown without her name, or shown with it.

[do: approve-points]
Only what she approves leaves the conversation. When researchers ran AI interviews like this with several hundred people, ninety-nine percent approved the summary of their own views. Here, approval isn't a statistic. It's the gate.

## The picture {#picture}

[do: open-picture]
Now, and only now, she sees everyone else. This is the picture: what forty-nine people in her town have said, grouped by what it's about.

[do: point-her-theme]
Her point about classes running while she's asleep sits here, with eighteen other people. The name of the theme is a phrase one of them used.

[do: open-sources]
Every sentence in a summary can be opened. Here are the words behind it, and the number of people. The counting is done by ordinary code, never by the AI.

[do: point-not-what-was-said]
If a summary gets it wrong, anyone can say so, right here. That report is shown beside the summary, with what was done about it.

[do: point-who-is-missing]
And the picture is honest about who's missing. Only three people under twenty-five have been heard so far. Ten were hoped for.

## Statements and reasons {#statements}

[do: open-statements]
From the picture, Unite drafts statements people can agree or disagree with. Each one lists the points it rests on.

[do: react-statement]
Amina answers first, and only then sees how others answered. Experiments on the wisdom of crowds show why: when people see each other's answers first, the answers bunch together without getting any better.

[do: open-differ]
Some statements are common ground. Some aren't, and Unite publishes those just as carefully. This one split the room: nobody should lose pay to retrain.

[do: point-reasons]
Under every statement are reasons for and reasons against, side by side, in the order they arrived. No likes. No replies to people. Just the argument.

## Working it through {#room}

[do: open-room]
When people really differ, a few of them are drawn by lot into a live room. Six people who see it differently, and forty-five minutes.

[do: room-messages]
The facilitator is an AI. It keeps time and turns, and it invites the people who haven't spoken. It never takes a side and never sums anyone up. In one study on Stanford's online deliberation platform, nearly a thousand people met in rooms run this way.

[do: room-outcome]
An earlier room came back with new wording: paid learning hours, with the cost shared between the employer and the skills fund. That version became common ground.

[do: slow-room]
Not everyone can meet at a set time. So a room can also be slow: open for days, where you write where you stand before you can read the others.

[do: talk-it-through]
And anyone can talk a statement through in private, with the AI as a go-between. It shows what people who answered differently said, in their own words, and asks what wording you could accept.

## Deciding, and what comes first {#decide}

[do: open-statement-rule]
The rule for deciding was fixed in the pledge, before anyone spoke. And on every statement Unite says, in plain words, what's still needed for it to pass.

[do: open-priorities]
The moment statements pass, everyone is asked the next question: which three matter most to you?

[do: choose-priorities]
Amina picks hers, and the list counts her in. It's ordered so that once a large group's first choice is placed, their other choices count for less. That way smaller groups reach the top as well.

[do: point-capacity]
The top five go to the council and the college, who have until the eighteenth of December to answer each one.

## The host answers {#answer}

[do: open-volunteering]
To see what happens next, here's a second example that's further along. A network of volunteer centres asked what a shared volunteering tool should do.

[do: open-decisions]
Five decisions came out of it. The host had to answer every one with one of four words: accepted, changed, delayed, or refused. And give reasons.

[do: open-refused]
This one was refused. The network won't pay travel costs through the tool, because there's no budget for it. A refusal with reasons is still an answer. Silence isn't.

## From a decision to a working tool {#deliver}

[do: open-decision-one]
This one was accepted. A volunteer proves who they are once, and can share that proof with any group they choose.

[do: highlight-tests]
Every decision carries numbered tests for "done", written so the people who made it can check them.

[do: open-proposal]
Builders proposed how to meet those tests, and the people who made the decision confirmed the plan was faithful to it. For software, AI coding agents can do much of the building, with a named person accountable for every piece.

[do: open-check]
Then it's checked, by a panel drawn by lot. Not by the host. Two tests met, and one not. So the record says: delivered, with small changes.

## The register {#register}

[do: open-register]
Every decision, in every assembly, lands in one public register. Delivered with small changes. In progress. Delayed. Refused.

[do: point-in-progress]
"In progress" is never counted as done. After France's climate convention, the government counted about two thirds of the measures as implemented in full or in part. An independent count found about one in five implemented as proposed. So here the categories are fixed in advance, and someone else does the counting.

[do: open-me]
And Amina has her own page: what happened to what I said. Each of her points, traced to the theme it joined and the statements that rest on it.

## Listening to the world {#listening}

[do: open-listening]
Not everything starts with a host. Open listening is always on. Anyone, anywhere, can have the conversation about their own life, in their own language.

[do: point-languages]
What they approve joins a wider picture. These are people in fourteen countries. All invented for this example.

[do: point-no-host]
Unite shows which themes keep coming up with nobody yet responsible for them. This one kept coming up: looking after an ageing parent while holding down a job.

[do: open-ai-and-care]
That's the opening for someone to step forward. Here a coalition of care workers, providers and family carers has drafted a pledge. It's published, and for nine more days anyone can challenge the question before it opens.

## Tell us what's wrong {#feedback}

[do: open-feedback]
Unite has to live by its own rules. On every page there's a button to give feedback, and it works the same way: a conversation.

[do: feedback-answer]
It never argues and never defends the product. What you've said so far is always on screen, and you can change it.

[do: open-your-feedback]
Afterwards you get a private link that shows what happened to your feedback, and why. The people who run Unite are a host like any other. Their pledge, still a draft, says they answer by a date, and someone else checks.

## What comes next {#outro}

[do: open-how]
Everything you've seen is open: the rules, the prompts the AI is given, and the record of every assembly.

[do: open-home]
What Unite needs now is a first real question, with a real host who's ready to promise an answer. If that could be you, start a conversation.

[then: end-card]
