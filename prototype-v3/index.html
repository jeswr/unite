About this demo

What this demo is, and is not (fictional data, English only, browser-only storage, no voting or AI)
  • Everything seeded is fictional: people, expert roles, responses, costs, dates and commitments. No real institution or organisation is shown, takes part or endorses this.
  • Open to everyone in ambition, not yet in practice. The demo is in English only, needs a browser and assumes reading and typing. Other languages, assisted and offline ways of taking part are future work, not features.
  • It runs only in this browser. Nothing is sent to a community, a server or an analytics service.
  • What you type is kept in this browser’s local storage so it survives a reload. Local storage is not encrypted; anyone using this browser profile could read it. Avoid entering anything you would not want stored that way.
  • There is no AI, no sign-in, no voting and no check that participants are real or unique people.
  • Agreement is a clue, not a verdict. It can point to workable proposals. It does not prove something is true or representative, and it never overrides the needs of people who disagree.

A shared space for futures

How Unite works

Unite is a place for anyone, anywhere, to take part in designing the future: to say what life they want, to learn what others want, and to turn shared hopes into things people can actually try or ask for.

Hopes about learning, care, work, nature, home and fairness are open to discussion by everyone. Acting on them is different: every proposal names who can act, with what authority and money, and when they must answer. Taking part is voluntary, so what the space hears is never a representative sample of any place or population.

Three kinds of outcome, kept apart

  • Aspirations are for discussion. Nobody has to agree, and nobody is bound by them.
  • Community experiments are small things a named group can fund and try itself, then report on.
  • Recommendations ask an institution to act. They change nothing unless that institution adopts and funds them.

The steps

  1. Describe the future you want

    In your own words, privately first. You choose whether to share.

  2. Explore others’ aspirations

    Read what others hope for and note what inspired you.

  3. Develop alternatives

    Costs, trade-offs and who might lose out, including keeping things as they are.

  4. Ask the experts

    Experts, with their interests disclosed, advise on what is feasible. People affected set the priorities.

  5. Connect ideas to action

    Who decided, why, with what money, and whether it was delivered and evaluated.

          Take it with you

          Export the public record

          The export is a versioned JSON file with stable record identifiers. It includes the proposal and options, every response and concern, the evidence and questions, commitments, any decision and the delivery trail, plus descriptions you chose to add to the demo community.

          It never includes your private drafts or saved bookmarks.

          Open the file in the separate read-only reader to see the same record rendered from the file alone. The reader shares this codebase, so it is a second view, not an independent implementation. The file is a plain, documented JSON format for this demo; it is not a published interchange standard.

          Files are versioned. Exports from the earlier demo example (version 1, amounts in euros) are refused rather than shown as this example.
