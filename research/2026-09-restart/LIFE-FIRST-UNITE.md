# Unite: begin with the lives people want

Research and design proposal · 27 September 2026

**Unite should help people describe the lives they want, discover what would make those lives possible together, and progressively change the arrangements that stand in the way.** Income, jobs, companies, states and even Unite itself are possible means. None belongs in the definition of success.

This updates the earlier [conversational proposal](CONVERSATIONAL-UNITE.md). Its employment-centred future scenarios are superseded as the product's starting point. The live discussion, participant review, visible disagreement and action follow-through remain useful. Read [A day we could make possible](A-DAY-WE-COULD-MAKE-POSSIBLE.md) for an accessible fictional outcome.

## What the research supports

This is a focused, interdisciplinary narrative review, not a systematic review or a claim to have read every cited book. Sources were checked on 27 September 2026. Research papers, authors' writing, research institutions and projects' own documentation take priority. Book descriptions are identified as such. Frameworks suggest design methods; empirical studies support narrower findings; political and economic visions remain proposals. None demonstrates a globally successful, leaderless, post-scarcity society.

### 1. Understand lived experience before designing its machinery

Human-centred design involves understanding people and contexts, evaluating designs with them, and iterating throughout a system's life. The relevant ISO standard provides a process foundation; its public description does not establish that any particular political arrangement is desirable. Unite needs research with people in their settings, not just usability tests of its own interface. [ISO 9241-210:2019, public abstract](https://committee.iso.org/standard/77520.html?browse=tc).

Experience design offers a closer starting point than a list of proposed policies. Hassenzahl and colleagues place meaningful and pleasurable moments at the centre of design, and use experience patterns to connect artefacts to what matters in those moments. For Unite, ask about cooking with someone, feeling safe outdoors, learning a craft or resting without guilt before asking which service to build. This is a design approach, not evidence that one experience pattern fits all cultures. [Hassenzahl et al., 2013](https://ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/1480/589).

The Day Reconstruction Method grounds inquiry in episodes of a recent day. Its original study compared reports from 909 employed women with established experience-sampling results. Unite can borrow the attention to concrete episodes, while acknowledging that an informal AI conversation is not the validated instrument and an imagined day is not a forecast of satisfaction. Pair an imagined good day with an optional recent-day account. [Kahneman et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15576620/).

Anthropic's interview project is particularly relevant: its report describes respondents moving from requests for productivity to the time and relationships productivity could enable. The 80,508 interviews were with self-selecting Claude users, not a representative sample of humanity. The useful lesson is the follow-up that uncovers purpose, not adoption of its categories as a universal needs taxonomy. [Anthropic's study](https://www.anthropic.com/features/81k-interviews). Its methodology combines an agreed interview guide, adaptive questioning and researchers' analysis; a conversational interface alone does not supply methodological rigour. [About Anthropic Interviewer](https://www.anthropic.com/about-anthropic-interviewer).

### 2. Separate ends, opportunities and ways of providing them

Max-Neef, Elizalde and Hopenhayn distinguish needs from the historically and culturally specific ways they are satisfied. This is useful for separating belonging from a particular club, or security from a particular employment contract. Their finite, universal classification of needs is a theoretical commitment, not something Unite should impose as settled fact. Use the distinction; let participants challenge the categories. [Human Scale Development, 1989, particularly printed pp. 17–38](https://www.daghammarskjold.se/wp-content/uploads/1989/05/89_1.pdf).

The capabilities approach asks what people are actually able to do and be, including their freedom to choose, rather than treating resources or purchases as sufficient measures. A vehicle is not mobility if someone cannot board it. Available food is not nourishment if it is unusable, culturally inappropriate or inaccessible. An opportunity matters even when someone chooses not to take it. [UNDP's human-development measurement primer, introduction](https://hdr.undp.org/system/files/documents/primercomplete.pdf).

Positive Design distinguishes pleasure, personal significance and virtue in a framework for flourishing. It helps prevent the product goal collapsing into a single pleasant-feeling score. Its moral vocabulary is itself open to debate; Unite should ask people what a worthwhile life means to them. [Desmet and Pohlmeyer, 2013](https://www.ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/1666).

METUX applies self-determination theory to technology and distinguishes effects at the interface, task, behaviour and life levels. Autonomy, competence and relatedness offer useful evaluation lenses. A person enjoying Unite while becoming less able to act outside it would be a failure of the product's purpose. These lenses supplement participants' definitions of wellbeing; they do not replace them. [Peters, Calvo and Ryan, 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00797/full).

### 3. Make values and power part of the design

Value Sensitive Design distinguishes designer values, stakeholder values and the values explicitly supported by a system. It combines conceptual, empirical and technical investigations, includes people indirectly affected, and examines how technology and social arrangements shape each other. Unite must state its own commitments—such as consent and freedom to disagree—and let people inspect how ranking, summaries and permissions enact them. It cannot honestly claim to be value-free. [University of Washington VSD Lab](https://vsdesign.org/vsd/).

Co-design changes the participant's role from a source of requirements to a creative partner. Unite should let people change the question, sketch alternatives and challenge the process itself. Having thousands of people answer a founder's fixed questionnaire is still a centrally framed exercise. [Sanders and Stappers, 2008, author-uploaded paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235700862_Co-creation_and_the_new_landscapes_of_design).

Participatory design's work on "infrastructuring" treats continuing relationships and opportunities for participation as part of what is designed. That suggests persistent capacity to organise, revisit decisions and maintain shared resources, beyond a launch or one-off consultation. [Björgvinsson, Ehn and Hillgren, 2012, author-uploaded paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/239857060_Design_Things_and_Design_Thinking_Contemporary_Participatory_Design_Challenges).

The Design Justice Network's community-authored principles foreground the people affected, community control, existing local practices and accountability for outcomes. Their implication here is substantive: accessibility, facilitation and the ability to set agendas need resources and power, not a token invitation. [Design Justice Network](https://designjustice.org/read-the-principles).

Escobar's *Designs for the Pluriverse* argues for place-based autonomy and multiple ways of living, informed by decolonial struggles. The publisher's description supports this broad orientation; this review is not a close reading of the book. Unite should allow compatible differences between communities instead of translating everyone into one Western individualist description of a good life. [Duke University Press, 2018](https://dukeupress.edu/designs-for-the-pluriverse).

### 4. Open the future, then work backwards

Speculative design uses imagined worlds to make assumptions available for discussion. A fictional outcome should invite rejection and amendment rather than advertise a future as inevitable. Dunne and Raby explicitly distinguish this practice from prediction. [*Speculative Everything*, publisher description](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262548687/speculative-everything/). Their later essay also warns that demanding a plausible route immediately can confine imagination to variations on the present. Unite can give imagination room before introducing implementation constraints. [Dunne and Raby, 2025, author excerpt](https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/what-designers-can-learn-from-magic-realism/).

Transition Design is especially close to this project's purpose: everyday life, long horizons, connected systems, locally grounded futures and multiple interventions. Its visions can change as people learn. Backcasting works from a desired situation toward enabling conditions and present steps. It is a discipline for inquiry, not a guarantee of transition. [Transition Design framework](https://staging.transitiondesign.net/about/), [CMU's Transition Design Institute](https://transitiondesigninstitute.net/).

Systemic design asks people to explore, reframe, create and sustain change, including relationships and activities that sit outside the visible product. This supports asking whether a barrier comes from an allocation rule or institutional arrangement, rather than always building a new app to cope with it. [Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/systemic-design-framework/).

Meadows distinguishes changing system parameters from changing rules, goals and paradigms, and explicitly cautions against treating the hierarchy as a recipe. Unite should keep both useful immediate relief and deeper changes visible. Helping someone pay for access and changing the rule that makes access conditional can be connected interventions. [Meadows, *Leverage Points*](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/).

### 5. Coordination without one permanent centre

vTaiwan combines online and offline participation, expert input and deliberation; its own description ends in legislation and involves existing public institutions. The English page is last updated December 2023. It is a partial precedent for consultation and institutional connection, not evidence that Taiwan has removed central decision makers. [vTaiwan's account](https://info.vtaiwan.tw/).

Polis identifies patterns of agreement and disagreement across participants. Its documentation also states what it does not do: choose the best idea or compare alternatives head-to-head. Opinion clustering does not establish population representativeness, delivery capacity or permission to act. Unite could learn from its cross-group visibility while retaining richer discussion elsewhere. [Computational Democracy Project FAQ](https://compdemocracy.org/faq/).

*Plurality* explores technology that enables collaboration across differences, including augmented deliberation and adaptive administration. These are valuable design proposals for differentiated, responsive coordination, not proof of a completed replacement for governments. [Augmented deliberation](https://plurality.net/read/5-4/), [Adaptive administration](https://plurality.net/read/5-5/).

Ostrom's research challenges the assumption that shared resources always require either a single state authority or privatisation. Self-governance involves rules, accountability and institutions; it is not the disappearance of governance. Its successful bounded cases should not be casually extrapolated to planetary coordination. [Ostrom's Nobel lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/lecture/).

Benkler analyses collaboration through commons-based peer production, where contributions can be self-selected rather than assigned through prices or managerial command. The strong examples concern information and cultural production with suitable distributed resources. Physical infrastructure, care and scarce materials require additional arrangements. [Benkler, *Coase's Penguin*](https://www.benkler.org/CoasesPenguin.html).

AI abundance is a legitimate scenario to investigate. Amodei's *Machines of Loving Grace* offers an optimistic account of possible gains in health and material wellbeing, with uncertainty and bottlenecks. It is a prospective essay by an AI developer, not empirical proof that scarcity or unequal access will end. Unite's distinctive question is how people could collectively direct and share gains if they arrive, and still improve lives if they arrive unevenly. [Amodei, 2024](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace).

## A precise design principle

**Keep the person's language; loosen the assumed mechanism.** We cannot eliminate all abstractions: “needs”, “health” and “happiness” are abstractions too. We can make interpretations explicit, provisional, revisable and connected to concrete experience.

If someone says “I need more income,” do not correct them or silently replace it with “access”. Ask what that would make possible. Retain both their statement and their confirmed explanation. Their current need for money may be urgent and entirely real. Exploring a world without that dependency should enlarge their options, not postpone help.

| What someone says | A useful next question | Possible underlying experience, only if confirmed | Open implementation space |
|---|---|---|---|
| “I want more income.” | “What would become possible in your everyday life?” | A secure home; unhurried meals; choices without fear | Money now, access guarantees, shared provision, different ownership, lower resource requirements |
| “Travel is too expensive.” | “Tell me about a journey you want to make and what stops it.” | Time with a loved one, exploration, independence | Fare help, local connection, accessible transport, different geography; no assumed substitute |
| “I need a better job.” | “What would a better day give you?” | Mastery, company, recognition, security—or work itself | Changed conditions, a chosen project, study, rest, different provision of essentials |
| “My health stops me.” | “What would you like to be able to do, with whatever support you would want?” | Participation, comfort, freedom, reliable support | Accessible settings, assistance, clinical care or research where chosen; no presumption that everyone seeks a cure |
| “Government should fix this.” | “What should change, and who experiences the consequences?” | Dependable provision and a meaningful say | Current agencies, community arrangements, distributed networks, new forms people devise |

This table offers prompts, not an automated relabelling dictionary. People may reject every interpretation. Relational obligations, spiritual practices, place and collective identities must fit without being forced into individual preference scores.

## The conversation Unite should offer

Start with: **“Imagine an ordinary day you would love to live—not a special occasion. What is happening in one moment of that day?”** If this feels difficult or painful, offer a recent moment they would keep, or a small change that would make tomorrow gentler. No forced optimism, full-day diary or mandatory disclosure.

Over several turns, following the person's lead:

1. **Describe:** What happens, who is around, how does it feel, what do you do or choose not to do? Ask one question at a time.
2. **Understand:** What makes that matter? Reflect an interpretation and let them correct it. Ask what is already good and should survive change.
3. **Explore barriers:** What gets in the way? Possible prompts include physical surroundings, health, social expectations, permission, knowledge, trust, time, access, support or scarce resources. These are optional lenses, not a compulsory taxonomy.
4. **Open possibilities:** If the barrier disappeared, what else would they choose? Would their preferred experience require today's institution? Can they imagine another route? Do not make belief in AI or abundance a condition of participation.
5. **Connect:** What would other people need for this to work? Whose cooperation and consent matter? What might be a shared aim and what should remain different?
6. **Confirm:** Return the person's own account, interpretations, unknowns and boundaries. No AI inference becomes their preference without confirmation.
7. **Choose a next step:** Keep it private, share an edited fragment, find others exploring it, examine a possibility, or leave. Participation creates no work obligation.

Separate discovery from inspiration: first elicit an unprompted account; then offer varied, clearly fictional examples; afterwards ask whether these changed or narrowed what the person imagined. That helps detect anchoring rather than mistaking it for agreement.

Private reflection must never automatically become public testimony, eligibility evidence or a social score. Health experiences can be discussed without collecting diagnoses. Optional support-finding is a distinct role from research and from clinical advice. The interviewer should not diagnose, prescribe or promise cures.

## The optimistic destination

Imagine a world where everyone can reliably obtain the material conditions, relationships and support needed to pursue a life they value. AI and automation take on increasing amounts of unwanted labour, help discover treatments and designs, and reduce the coordination burden. Access to essentials no longer depends on selling labour. People choose care, craft, exploration, friendship, study and rest on their own terms.

People and communities continually express what is working and what is missing. With permission, tools connect related needs, reveal shared opportunities and expose conflicting consequences. People can propose alternatives, test them, scrutinise their effects and revise the arrangements. There is no required lifelong profession, compulsory participation or single official good life.

In one ambitious version, present-day governments have evolved into networks of limited, revisable functions. Communities author agreements; affected people decide changes; technical systems coordinate provision under those agreements; disputes have independent review. There is no permanent centre entitled to decide humanity's future. Some functions still need named responsibility, bounded delegation and dependable emergency response. Removing all accountable decision procedures would let hidden power reappear in platform operators, infrastructure owners or whoever can act fastest.

Unite would be one replaceable interface to this collaborative capacity. People could use different clients, models, local practices and offline assemblies while exchanging records they understand and consent to share. Owning the model, codebase or hosting service would confer no right to set the collective goal.

This is an original normative scenario, not a research result. It assumes major progress in physical production, energy, health, safe automation, shared access and cooperation. Lower production cost alone does not guarantee fair distribution. Some goods may remain scarce or impossible; time, ecological space and another person's willing attention cannot simply be manufactured. A good design keeps these questions open without using today's shortages to close imagination prematurely.

## From personal wishes to a world that works together

The missing middle is an explicit chain of reasoning:

**Lived moment → participant-confirmed purpose → barriers → several possible arrangements → affected people and dependencies → chosen experiment → observed change in life → revised vision.**

Each link should be challengeable. Sources belong with claims. Distinguish the participant's words, the AI's interpretation, an expert's feasibility claim, a speculative assumption and an observed result. “Common ground” is a provisional area of overlap, not permission to erase incompatible aspirations.

One shared vision can mean common guarantees with many different lives. A resident wants quiet evenings; a musician wants a lively gathering. Separate sites, suitable spaces or time agreements may satisfy both better than voting for one lifestyle. If no acceptable accommodation exists, show the conflict and decide it through an agreed process; do not rename it consensus.

Experts contribute competing, attributable assessments of feasibility, evidence, risks and uncertainty. People remain the authority on their own experience; neither majority preference nor lived experience alone settles a technical claim. Invite disability and community knowledge alongside professional expertise. Disclose interests, invite disagreement between experts, and commission tests of disputed assumptions.

For real decisions, record the affected group, participation gaps, relevant rights, consent boundaries, resource commitments, who is responsible, review date and appeal route. A large online audience is not automatically that group. Individuals decide personal matters; affected communities decide shared arrangements; wider consequences require wider participation. Routine execution can be delegated within revocable limits. Nobody should need to spend all day governing in order to receive necessities.

Open conversation and binding decisions need different participation arrangements. An open feed discovers issues; targeted outreach finds missing affected people; supported deliberation can examine alternatives; the relevant community decides through a process it has authorised. Sampling and rotating panels are possible tools where continuous participation is impractical, not automatic claims to speak for everyone. Translation and synthesis need correction routes in the original language. Children, future generations, nonparticipants and ecological effects need explicit representation appropriate to the decision.

Protocols and standards also distribute power. Publish proposed meanings and changes; allow multiple implementations and compatible extensions; make migration possible; record who can revise decision procedures and how those revisions can be challenged. A forkable codebase is insufficient if one identity provider, model, resource owner or definition of a valid record remains unavoidable. Federation itself is a design hypothesis to test against coordination costs and unequal capacity.

The system should help people change conditions, not manipulate them into wanting less so its happiness score rises. Learning and reflection may change preferences, but that should happen transparently and voluntarily. Success must include freedom, relationships and the ability to refuse, not only reported pleasure. No personal life model should become a compulsory digital twin authorised to vote or consent in someone's absence.

### A proposed operating loop

This is a design proposal to test, not a mechanism established by the literature:

1. A person can seek help with one experience immediately, without agreeing to participate in a global planning process. Sharing with a small trusted group and publishing globally are distinct choices.
2. Related accounts can suggest a shared opportunity. People confirm the connection; similarity of wording is insufficient. Preserve unusual and urgent needs that do not form large clusters.
3. Participants create at least several materially different options where possible, including changing an arrangement rather than only adding a product. Keep a branch for imaginative possibilities whose prerequisites are not yet available.
4. Providers, maintainers, experts and affected non-users identify dependencies and burdens. Record an actual offer separately from hypothetical capacity. AI cannot volunteer another person's labour or resources.
5. The appropriate group chooses what to test through agreed rules. Personal consent remains personal; large majorities do not manufacture it. Unresolved rights or significant external effects require their own process.
6. A responsible team carries out the bounded experiment with a fallback if it fails. The system follows commitments and prompts review; it does not turn a generated plan into an automatic deployment.
7. Participants describe what changed in their lives, including disappointments and unexpected effects. Compare that with the intended experience, update the shared evidence and reopen the design if needed.

Keep an immediate-help queue and a longer transformation queue connected to the same purposes. A small solution must not make the structural barrier disappear from view. Equally, a distant transformation must not become a reason to withhold available help. A short list of priorities should disclose the reasons for ordering it; the prototype's arithmetic score cannot settle whose unmet needs deserve resources.

## Working backwards into the present

Use two connected views: a desired-life view that can imagine fundamental change, and a present-action view that honestly names existing prices, laws, ownership and capacity. They refer to the same purpose. Present budgets are implementation facts, not the vocabulary of the destination.

| Stage | What Unite helps people do | Evidence needed to proceed |
|---|---|---|
| Understand life | Conduct voluntary conversations and offline co-design; confirm interpretations | People recognise their own meaning; important experiences are not systematically omitted |
| Try a better moment | Match a specific barrier with an available resource or voluntary group | A real improvement, no hidden burden shifted to someone else, ongoing consent |
| Change a recurring arrangement | Compare several ways to deliver the same capability; jointly govern a sustained service or resource | Reliability, accessibility, resource accounting, conflict handling and accountability |
| Share gains from automation | Decide who owns, accesses and can change productive tools; track reduced burden and unmet needs | Benefits reach people beyond owners and frequent participants; providers can be replaced |
| Connect across communities | Coordinate shared resources and external effects through interoperable agreements | Independent implementations work; excluded groups have access; cross-boundary disputes can be resolved |
| Rework institutions | Retain, transform or replace functions as people choose and alternatives earn trust | No loss of essential provision or rights during transition; legitimate adoption and workable exit |

These are revisitable conditions, not a universal sequence or timeline. Useful starting points can arise within a household, cooperative, library, care organisation, village, research network, business or public body. Work with existing institutions where they help; do not define success as making everyone join them. Research and testing can reveal that an imagined arrangement should be revised or abandoned.

The transition needs physical resources and people even when writing software is cheap. Keep AI development credits separate from facilitation, translation, accessibility, inference at scale and actual provision. No new large software budget is assumed here. No prospective provider or funder is represented as committed.

## Assessment of the current prototype, before this revision

The inspected code is `prototype-v4/` at commit `825466e`. Its foundation supports a small conversation-and-action experiment, but it does not yet support the full transition above.

| Existing design | Assessment | Necessary direction |
|---|---|---|
| Live chronological posts and replies | Useful social connection; no evidence yet of improved deliberation | Connect posts to experiences, support alternative viewpoints and evaluate harassment/exclusion |
| Private, opt-in AI interview and reviewed publishing | Strong starting boundary; opening and guide too general | Begin with a concrete lived moment; invite correction; keep mechanisms provisional |
| Work/care/climate rooms and employment scenarios | Frames participants inside today's institutions before discovery | Lead with daily experience; treat institutional models as optional later material |
| One common-ground statement per room | Preserves some disagreement but compresses many needs into one proposition | Eventually support multiple purposes, alternative arrangements and unresolved tensions |
| Support/concern/abstain and version history | Useful local response record | Never equate it with consensus of all affected people or automatic authority |
| One-number priority score | Convenient queue, insufficient for legitimate allocation | Keep urgent unmet needs, rights, burdens, affected groups and dependencies visible; no universal happiness maximisation |
| Owner-reviewed actions and check-in date | Provides accountability for a task, not proof that life improved | State desired lived change, how participants will recognise it and who may be harmed |
| Public JSON export | A limited portability step | Independent clients/operators, interoperable records, repairable access and participant governance |
| One server, one AI provider, in-memory state, English | Local demonstration only | Durable and consent-aware records, multilingual/offline participation, independent evaluation and shared control |

Changing copy and prompts can correct the entry point. It does not implement equitable resource allocation, federated governance or global needs discovery. The accompanying implementation report must keep that boundary explicit.

## What to learn next

Run a small, consented learning cycle with people who differ in language, disability, available time, resources and confidence about the future. This is exploratory co-design, not representative democracy. Include people who do not want AI and people unable or unwilling to use the website. Arrange accessible participation and compensation for research time without making support conditional on contribution.

Compare an open daily-life invitation with a policy-first invitation, using a counterbalanced design where practical. Have independent human reviewers and participants examine summaries, not only another model. Track whether the system suggests money, jobs, government or a post-scarcity ideology before the participant does. Test culturally varied accounts and contradictory preferences. A prompt can ask for neutrality; it cannot prove neutrality.

Measure whether people feel correctly understood and able to correct the system; whether their options widen; whether shared plans preserve dissent; whose needs go unanswered; whether a chosen real-world experiment changes the experience it targeted; whether benefits and burdens are distributed fairly; and whether people can stop using Unite without losing access to support. Report disagreement and missing data, not only averages.

The leading outcome is **more people able to live days they have reason to value, on terms they can help shape**. Time on site, agreement rates, generated proposals and tasks completed are supporting observations, never adequate substitutes.
