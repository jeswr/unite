# Unite: from imagining a better future to making it happen

Research and first operating proposal • 26 September 2026 • Version 0.1

**Recommendation:** build Unite as a network of communities that turn people's aspirations into accountable experiments and decisions. Start with one funded Solid-community challenge, then test the process in a community outside technology. Earn broader influence through useful outcomes, fair participation and independently usable infrastructure.

This is a fresh proposal, informed by precedents and the existing repository. Statements about existing projects have sources; proposed mechanisms, targets and budgets are our hypotheses. No partner, expert, funding, public mandate or participant demand has been secured by this research.

## What to preserve, and what to reconsider

The original idea has three valuable commitments: people can articulate their own futures; expertise helps make those futures achievable; and nobody should acquire an exclusive right to mediate that process. Keep all three.

Reconsider the assumption that everybody must adopt one social platform, that convergence is always desirable, or that building applications is the easy part. People will have incompatible preferences and unequal power. A working application also needs maintenance, accessibility, security, support and someone accountable when it fails. Generating code reduces some costs; it does not provide those institutions.

The credible promise is: **“Describe something you want to change. Help shape a practical response. See who will act and what happens next.”** The global ambition can remain, while each individual invitation has a bounded purpose and an honest account of its influence.

The July repository already articulates dissent, federation, expert input and action trails. Its v2 design explicitly describes a vision-selling prototype. This proposal does not claim those ideas are new; it makes partner commitments, delivery resources, evidence and evaluation the organizing structure, and reopens design choices the earlier plan treated as fixed. Repository reference: [existing design](../../design/v2/README.md).

## What the precedents actually establish

| Precedent | Useful contribution | Limit for Unite |
|---|---|---|
| [Polis](https://compdemocracy.org/faq/) | Short statements and agree/disagree/pass responses can reveal shared ground across opinion groups. | Its own FAQ says it is not a best-idea selector and cautions against groups below roughly 50–100. Participation and clusters do not establish population representation. |
| [vTaiwan](https://info.vtaiwan.tw/) | Combines online input, facilitated discussion and institutional engagement through proposal, opinion, reflection and legislation stages. | The website's outcome claims are practitioner reports, with the page marked last updated December 2023. Do not treat reported success rates as independent causal evidence. |
| [Decidim](https://docs.decidim.org/en/develop/features/general-description.html) | Existing open software for proposals, assemblies, processes and accountability. | Software installation does not secure decision-making authority, inclusive recruitment or delivery money. |
| [CONSUL Democracy](https://consuldemocracy.org/) | Participation, proposals, budgeting, voting and collaborative legislative drafting. | Evaluate an adapter or partnership before duplicating mature participation infrastructure. |
| [Loomio](https://www.loomio.com/) | Group deliberation with explicit decisions, reasons and a retrievable outcome. | A consenting organization's decision procedure does not automatically confer public legitimacy. |
| [Ireland's Citizens' Assemblies](https://citizensassembly.ie/about/faq/) | Random selection, learning, facilitation and an explicit route to parliament and government. | Recommendations are advisory; institutions retain their legal roles. Public consideration and response are promised in advance. |
| [Participatory budgeting](https://pbhandbook.participatorybudgeting.org/) and [Reykjavík](https://reykjavik.is/en/my-neighborhood) | Community ideas become feasible options, funding decisions and implementation. | A bounded budget and an implementing institution are part of the intervention. Popularity alone cannot supply either. |
| [UNESCO Futures Literacy](https://www.unesco.org/en/futures-literacy/resources) | Methods for exploring assumptions and alternative futures. | Imagination needs a separate route to commitments and present-day action. |

These examples support assembling useful practices. They do not demonstrate that one global system can fairly aggregate everyone's values or that Unite's particular combination will work. Practitioner websites are particularly useful for workflow descriptions, less reliable for claims of superiority or lasting impact.

**Differentiation to test:** a portable connection between personal aspirations, collective choices, expert evidence, institutional commitments and observed outcomes. That is a sharper contribution than another discussion board, voting site or universal needs taxonomy.

## The product people would use

Offer three levels of participation: five minutes to describe a need or react to an option; an hour to help investigate it; several sessions to develop and judge a response. Someone can benefit without becoming a governance enthusiast.

Begin with “Describe a better day.” Ask what is difficult now, what should change, what matters about that change, and what must not be sacrificed. Permit uncertainty, voice or assisted participation, and revision. Do not require people to classify their lives into a particular psychological model. Taxonomies are optional, versioned interpretations, not authoritative descriptions of human needs. Value Sensitive Design provides a useful discipline: investigate stakeholder values, real contexts and technical consequences together, including people affected indirectly. [VSD Lab](https://vsdesign.org/vsd/)

Keep an individual's notebook private by default. Sharing creates a deliberately chosen contribution for a particular audience. Public discovery can inspire people, but offer the chance to express their own view first. Record inspiration and attribution without exposing private source material.

The useful social objects are aspirations, questions, proposals and the projects people help make real. Let users follow a topic, community or project and receive an occasional update when something substantive changes. Recognize work and attribution without converting popularity, donations or time online into governing power.

Every challenge should have an understandable progression:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  A[Describe a better future] --> B[Understand needs and differences]
  B --> C[Develop and test options]
  C --> D[Record a legitimate decision]
  D --> E[Commit resources and deliver]
  E --> F[Measure change and revise]
  F --> A
```

The record connecting these steps is the core product. “Chosen,” “funded,” “built,” “used” and “beneficial” are distinct states. Show who made each claim and the supporting evidence. A reasoned rejection, unresolved disagreement, missed deadline or failed experiment must remain visible too.

## Psychology: enable cooperation without engineering conformity

The design objective should be informed agency, mutual understanding and workable joint action. A high consensus score is insufficient.

Two research results are relevant but bounded. Tessler and colleagues studied AI mediation with 5,734 participants and found participants preferred its group statements to human-mediator statements in the tested settings. That supports a carefully evaluated drafting assistant; it does not validate autonomous policy selection, durable legitimacy or performance across all cultures. [Research paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852)

Voelkel and colleagues tested 25 interventions with 32,059 U.S. participants. Many reduced partisan animosity, while fewer improved measures of antidemocratic attitudes. Friendlier conversations and democratic health are different outcomes. The results are not evidence that a particular Unite interface will succeed. [Research paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh4764)

Proposed design practices:

- Separate lived experience, underlying needs, factual claims and proposed solutions. People can share a need while disagreeing about the remedy.
- Ask for alternatives and a current-practice option. Discuss costs, uncertainty, distribution of benefits and potential harms before endorsement.
- Use small facilitated groups, independent reflection and equal opportunities to contribute. Participants can question the framing and request another witness.
- Preserve minority reports and unresolved objections beside every summary. A reasoned rights or safety concern triggers examination; it is neither automatically erased by a majority nor an unlimited veto.
- Have an author confirm any AI interpretation of their own contribution. Show sources, changes and omitted points. AI translations and summaries remain correctable.
- Make recommendation rules inspectable. Offer choices such as local relevance, new contributions and different perspectives. Never infer a covert psychological persuasion profile.
- Investigate disagreement rather than presuming the answer lies at a midpoint. Some factual claims have much stronger evidence; some conflicts concern rights, resources or irreconcilable values.

Recruit a paid social or political psychologist and a deliberation practitioner to co-design a research protocol. Measure comprehension, felt pressure to agree, accuracy of summaries, inclusion and follow-through. Test comprehension of privacy and authority claims before collecting sensitive real-life stories.

## How Unite connects to political and organizational decisions

**No consultation should launch without saying who can do what with its results.** OECD guidance emphasizes purpose, meaningful influence, inclusion and feedback; poorly implemented participation can damage trust. [Participation guidance](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-for-citizen-participation-processes_f765caf6-en/full-report/component-6.html)

Use a published process charter agreed by the commissioning body and community representatives. It names the question, affected population, powers and limits, available resources, recruitment, decision rule, conflicts of interest, publication policy, response deadline and appeal route. Fix the rule before seeing the result. Material changes require an explained amendment and, where necessary, renewed participation.

| Situation | Legitimate use of Unite | Required institutional connection |
|---|---|---|
| Solid or other open-source community | Discover needs, prioritize an agreed pilot budget, commission work | Maintainer and budget-holder commitment; explicit merge and maintenance responsibility |
| School, cooperative, union or nonprofit | Co-design a service or policy within delegated powers | Board or authorized body's agreed remit; affected members' rights |
| Local government | Input to a service decision, budget allocation or representative panel | Named officer and elected sponsor, lawful procedure, published response and delivery ownership |
| National policy | Public input and evidence for parliamentary or administrative processes | Formal sponsor, appropriate recruitment, expert scrutiny and jurisdiction-specific legal review |
| Private business | Customer/community design and impact assessment | Contractual commitment to respond; transparent interests; no claim to speak for the public |

Keep open participation distinct from representative judgment. Anyone can offer an experience; a self-selected group cannot be described as “what the public wants.” For higher-stakes public decisions, combine open submissions with an independently recruited, appropriately stratified civic lottery or another justified sampling method. Publish recruitment, attendance, attrition and missing voices. Pay participants and provide language, disability, caring and offline support. [Involve inclusion guidance](https://www.involve.org.uk/resources/knowledge-base/how-do-i-setup-citizens-assembly/4-inclusive)

Institutional adoption should proceed through a useful service pilot, then a repeatable departmental process, then an authorized standing procedure. Avoid assuming that popularity will naturally translate into political influence. For cross-border or intergenerational issues, document affected people outside the participating jurisdiction and include advocates or representatives with an explicit mandate; no participant can literally represent all future people.

## Turn ideas into work someone can deliver

Require a short project brief for each shortlisted option: need addressed, beneficiaries and excluded groups, alternatives, evidence, expected outcomes, cost range, dependencies, owner, maintenance plan, risks, measures and stop conditions.

Run a portfolio rather than a winner-takes-all tournament. A modest budget can support a few different experiments, including a minority-backed option that addresses a serious underserved need. Publish the allocation rule and all conflicts. Funders may set an openly declared program remit; they should not secretly change rankings or suppress unfavorable findings.

For a Solid pilot, the delivery sequence is user research → acceptance criteria → technical feasibility and security review → bounded implementation → user acceptance → maintained release → follow-up. Claude can implement approved software briefs and propose tests. Humans accountable to the pilot own approvals and acceptance. An autonomous agent does not receive authority to spend, merge or deploy just because a proposal has support.

For a public-service pilot, replace the software steps as needed with policy analysis, procurement, operational design and service delivery. A shared record can span both; the legal decision procedure and delivery method should be configurable, not forced into one global voting mechanism.

## Experts who are useful and accountable

Build a small paid roster around actual challenges, rather than an undifferentiated “expert council.” Distinguish subject specialists, practitioners who can deliver, people with lived experience, and independent reviewers. Academic credentials are evidence about a domain, not a license to decide others' priorities.

For each consequential claim, record the question, evidence source and date, confidence, known disagreement, expertise and interests of the contributor. Ask specialists what would change their judgment. Give participants plain-language answers and the opportunity to commission another view. Weight evidence by quality, rather than manufacturing equal airtime for unsupported claims. Involve's guidance gives a practical model for balanced evidence and participant questions. [Expert evidence guidance](https://www.involve.org.uk/resources/knowledge-base/how-do-i-setup-citizens-assembly/7-generative-learning)

Initial prospective collaborators, **not confirmed partners**: Involve or DemocracyNext for process design; the Computational Democracy Project for opinion mapping; a Value Sensitive Design/HCI group for values and interface research; a political-psychology research group for evaluation; accessibility organizations and Solid practitioners for the pilot. Recruit an independent evaluator early enough to influence the protocol, with the right to publish negative results.

## Decentralization must include the ability to leave

Multiple servers running one dominant implementation still leave concentrated power. Multiple codebases sharing one mandatory registry, identity issuer, model provider or licensing authority do too.

The proposed minimum common language describes aspirations, needs, questions, evidence, alternatives, concerns, decisions, commitments and observations. Include stable identifiers, revisions, language, attribution, audience, provenance and a process charter. Keep personal records and formal collective records separate. Decisions need a verifiable history; private drafts need deletion and correction. Do not put intimate personal data on an immutable public ledger.

[Solid](https://solidproject.org/TR/protocol) can provide permissioned access to externally stored data; the published document reviewed is a Community Group report, not a W3C Recommendation. [ActivityPub](https://www.w3.org/TR/activitypub/) provides a standardized federation building block. Neither establishes one-human-one-vote, representative recruitment, legal authority, semantic agreement or guaranteed deletion of copies held elsewhere. [ODRL](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/) describes permitted use; our architectural conclusion is that a policy statement alone cannot prevent misuse.

Adopt your data-model federation work where it earns its complexity: several communities can publish versioned profiles and documented mappings, with lossy translations visibly identified. An extension must not silently redefine a core field. Registries should be replaceable and mirrorable; existing records must remain interpretable when one operator disappears.

Require two genuinely independently maintained implementations and two independent operators before claiming that Unite has escaped dependence on a single codebase. Fund the second implementation explicitly. Sharing code is allowed, but a second skin over the same engine is not independent governance. Conformance fixtures should cover preserved dissent, audience boundaries, unknown versions, identifier conflicts and round-trip meaning.

Identity should be proportional to the decision: pseudonyms for exploratory discussion; process-scoped eligibility checks for scarce funds or formal decisions; stronger assurance only where justified. Separate eligibility from publicly displayed identity. WebIDs, emails, signatures and multiple servers do not by themselves prevent duplicate people or coordinated capture.

Unite will still need discovery, search, moderation, translation, indexing and operational funding. Make indexes nonexclusive, moderation decisions appealable and export useful. Decide explicitly which information can be cached, for how long, and what withdrawal means. A technically federated system can still be socially centralized.

## Govern the commons without pretending governance can disappear

Start with a small accountable organization or an appropriate fiscal sponsor to employ people, receive funds and contract with pilots. Separate that legal entity from the right to implement the protocol.

Proposed initial safeguards: published finances and decisions; an affected-community council with real budget and process powers; implementer/operator participation; independent research and rights expertise; term limits and declared conflicts; no single organization controlling a constitutional-change quorum. Founder authority should have a published scope and sunset. Donor contribution does not buy extra participant votes.

Publish proposals, objections and compatibility impacts for protocol changes. Ratify through multiple constituencies and observed adoption, permit competing profiles and preserve prior versions. Create an independent complaints route with review deadlines. These mechanisms require testing and legal drafting; they are not guarantees against capture.

Political opinions and inferences about them may be special-category data. The ICO states that UK processing requires both an Article 6 lawful basis and an Article 9 condition, and notes its guidance is under review after legislative change. Before a live UK pilot, map controllers and processors, complete the appropriate impact assessment and get advice for the actual use case. Federation does not remove this responsibility. [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-for-the-use-of-personal-data-in-political-campaigning-1/special-category-data/)

Do not require a public biography to participate. Avoid profiling children in the first pilot; any later youth participation needs a specifically designed safeguarding and consent process. Moderate harassment and coordinated disruption with published rules, local accountability and appeals. Retain the ability to discuss unpopular views without making abuse the price of participation.

## Adoption: a network of useful communities

The early customer is a convenor with a real problem, a trusted relationship with participants and some capacity to act. The early user's reward is influence over something they recognize, plus evidence that their time mattered.

Start in Solid because you have relevant context and access to implementers. Include people who need applications but do not build protocols. Otherwise the pilot tests an expert hobby community and teaches little about broader adoption. A second pilot should involve a less technical community and a different decision owner.

Distribute through organizations people already trust: community groups, libraries, cooperatives, schools, civil-society networks and existing collaboration tools. Offer assisted and offline participation, portable reports and embeds. Participants should not have to understand pods, cryptography or a federation before contributing.

Scale first by repeating one useful process, then training independent convenors, then supporting interoperable implementations. Permit long gaps between visits. A resident who returns when a relevant decision arises is a successful user; daily screen time is a poor mission metric.

All counts and costs below are planning assumptions, not market quotes or grant conditions. Proposed scale: 60–100 recruited contributors and a paid 12–16-person design group across users, maintainers, accessibility needs and relevant perspectives. This is a community pilot, not a statistically representative public assembly. Small subgroup estimates will be uncertain; do not publish misleading consensus percentages.

## How to know whether it worked

Pre-register evaluation questions and publish an anonymized results report, including negative outcomes. Use mixed methods. Proposed feasibility targets are decision rules for this pilot, not validated universal thresholds:

- At least 80% of tested participants correctly understand who sees a contribution and who holds decision authority; revise the interface if not.
- Every selected proposal has an owner, resources or explicit unfunded status, acceptance criteria and a response date. Publish all missed commitments.
- At least one agreed change is delivered and evaluated with affected users within the pilot's remit; measure benefit against a baseline, not merely delivery.
- At least 75% of surveyed contributors say the synthesis represents their view fairly; investigate the experiences of those who disagree and of people who drop out. Report denominators and nonresponse.
- Track recruitment and retention by relevant access needs and stakeholder groups without exposing sensitive identities. Small groups require qualitative analysis and privacy protection.
- Record author corrections, minority-view omissions, moderation appeals, meeting burden, cost per completed decision and whether a convenor voluntarily commits to another cycle.

For causal claims about AI or deliberation, use a sufficiently powered, independently designed comparison rather than a small pilot's before/after satisfaction score. Possible comparison: human facilitation with existing tools versus the same process with Unite assistance. The first pilot is primarily a feasibility and learning exercise.

Stop or redesign if the institution repeatedly ignores agreed response obligations, vulnerable participants experience unresolved harm, summaries systematically erase dissent, the process cannot recruit beyond insiders, or nobody wants a second cycle after seeing the cost. A smaller honest result is more valuable than an unearned claim to speak for humanity.
