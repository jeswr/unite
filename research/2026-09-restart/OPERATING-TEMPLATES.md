# Unite: templates for starting a real pilot

26 September 2026. These are proposed working documents, not signed agreements, legal instruments, confirmed partnerships or submitted applications. Replace every unresolved field before recruiting participants. The research basis and funding verification are in [the proposal](PROPOSAL.md).

## 1. A process charter people can understand

**Working question:** Which one or two improvements would most help non-specialists use community-owned Solid applications, within the resources of this pilot?

**Scope:** onboarding, accessibility, understandable sharing and provider choice. Participants may challenge this framing before the charter is agreed. A finding that a nontechnical intervention would help more than new software is an acceptable outcome.

Suggested participant statement: “Your contribution can influence the pilot described here. It does not authorize Unite to speak for you on other matters. You may disagree with a group summary, change your view, or leave. The decision owner will explain its response by the date above. If funding or authority changes, we will say so.”

Distinguish three outcomes before opening submissions: recommendations the sponsor can act on, matters it must refer elsewhere, and questions for which no implementation promise exists. Do not silently move an idea between those categories after participation.

## 3. An expert commission

**Question:** [One bounded, answerable question in plain language]

**Why this expertise:** [Relevant experience and its limits]

**Community context:** [Who needs an answer and what decision it informs]

**Requested output:** A two-page accessible note separating established evidence, uncertain estimates and value judgments; alternatives; possible harms; evidence that would change the answer; sources and conflicts.

**Exchange:** A short facilitated Q&A where participants can challenge assumptions and request clarification. An accessible recording or written transcript if consent and context allow.

**Compensation and independence:** [Fee and deadline]. Payment is for honest work, not support for a favored proposal. The expert may publish a disagreement. Advice does not replace participants' priorities or the lawful decision owner's responsibilities.

**Review:** [Another qualified reviewer for consequential technical claims; affected users for relevance and comprehensibility]

## 4. A funder concept to develop with a partner

**Working title: Unite — community decisions with a visible path to action.**

People are frequently asked what they think about the future of technology, while decisions about design, deployment and money remain elsewhere. Unite proposes an open process and interoperable tools through which communities can describe the futures they want, develop alternatives with expert help and hold named institutions accountable for responding and delivering.

The proposed first program joins practical community organizing with a bounded delivery fund. Participants help set the question, learn about alternatives and judge concrete proposals. Experts answer questions and explain uncertainty. Decision owners publish reasons, resource commitments and milestones. Participants can follow the result and contest summaries or decisions that misrepresent their contribution.

The platform's long-term public benefit is portability and institutional accountability: communities should be able to change software, hosts and service providers without losing their contributions, concerns or decision history. The initial prototype tests the interaction and record format. Production claims require independent implementation, privacy and security work, and observed use.

For an AI-focused program, a prospective U.S. partner could lead a pilot on how educators and a community want AI used in a specific service, or how workers want it governed in a particular workplace. Those are possible directions, not selected communities or established relationships. The partner must set the agenda with affected people and possess a credible route to change. The project should allow participants to reject an AI deployment or prefer a non-AI solution.

Proposed outputs are one completed decision cycle, a tested implementation or policy change, a portable public record, a documented participation method and an independently written evaluation. Outcome measures include whether people understand their influence and privacy choices, whether summaries preserve their views, who is excluded, whether promised action happens, and whether the community wants another cycle.

The ask should fund people as well as technology: participant compensation, access, facilitation, expert work, engineering, evaluation and maintenance. A 90-day Solid feasibility pilot is estimated at £100,000 in the proposal. A U.S. program needs a separately costed local budget, suitable grant duration and eligible lead; it should not copy that estimate or imply a currency conversion is a plan.

**Readiness gaps to resolve before a submission:** named community lead; evidence of an existing trusted relationship; agreed problem and authority; paid participant involvement in design; team availability; full budget; fiscal or legal arrangements; data responsibilities; evidence and evaluation plan. No claim that these are already secured.

## 6. Questions for participant research

Ask about actual behavior before showing the concept:

1. What is one thing you recently wanted to improve in a community or service, and what did you do?
2. Where did your idea go, and how did you know what happened to it?
3. Who had power to make the change? Who else was affected or missing?
4. What would make participation worth an hour of your time? What support would you need?
5. Which parts of your experience would you keep private, share with a group, or publish?
6. After trying the prototype: what has been saved, who can see it, and has any real person received it?
7. Who can decide here, what money is actually committed, and what could still fail?
8. Does the summary preserve your concern? What would make you distrust it or leave?

Avoid asking only whether people “like the idea.” Observe them complete a task, explain what it means, and identify an actual situation in which they would return.
